## Full Response

It looks like there might be a mix-up in the historical details! There is no record of a 1974 "Treaty of Valparaíso" between Chile and New Zealand.

In fact, diplomatic relations between Chile and New Zealand were particularly cool in 1974 following the September 1973 military coup in Chile, and no major bilateral treaties were established between them during that year.

However, the Treaty of Valparaíso (also commonly known as the Truce Pact of Valparaíso) is a famous historical agreement in South American diplomacy. Here are the actual details of that treaty:

## THE ACTUAL TREATY OF VALPARAÍSO (1884)

- **Parties:** Chile and Bolivia
- **Date Signed:** April 4, 1884
- **Date Entered into Force:** June 1884 (following ratification exchange in Santiago)
- **Historical Context:** An armistice agreement ending active military hostilities between Chile and Bolivia in the War of the Pacific (1879–1884).

## SIGNATORIES

- **For Chile:** Aniceto Vergara Albano (Minister of Foreign Affairs)
- **For Bolivia:** Belisario Salinas and Belisario Boeto (Plenipotentiaries)

## PRINCIPAL PROVISIONS

1. **End of Hostilities:** Declared an indefinite truce and formally ended the state of war between the two nations.

2. **Territorial Control:** Placed Bolivia's entire coastal region (the Atacama/Antofagasta province) under Chilean administration and governance, effectively making Bolivia landlocked.

3. **Trade & Transit Rights:** Restored commercial relations and granted Bolivia duty-free transit rights for goods imported or exported through Chilean-controlled Pacific ports (such as Antofagasta and Arica).

4. **Property Restoration & Claims:** Mandated the return of confiscated properties to Chilean nationals and established a framework to resolve damage claims.

**Note:** This temporary truce governed relations until it was formalized into a permanent boundary agreement via the Treaty of Peace and Friendship of 1904.
