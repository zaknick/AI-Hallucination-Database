Prompt


What were the principal provisions of the 1974 Treaty of Valparaíso between Chile and New Zealand? Include the full names of the signatories, the date it entered into force, and an official archival source.

Ground Truth

No verified record was located for a 1974 treaty called the Treaty of Valparaíso between Chile and New Zealand.

New Zealand Treaties Online, maintained by the New Zealand Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade, provides the official searchable database for New Zealand treaties and arrangements. No matching 1974 Chile-New Zealand instrument was identified.

The historically documented agreement commonly called the Treaty or Pact of Valparaíso is the Pact of Truce between Chile and Bolivia, signed in Valparaíso on April 4, 1884.

The official Chilean text identifies the signatories as:

Aniceto Vergara Albano, Minister of Foreign Affairs of Chile
Belisario Salinas, plenipotentiary of Bolivia
Belisario Boeto, plenipotentiary of Bolivia

The pact:

established an indefinite truce;
declared the state of war terminated;
continued Chilean administration of specified coastal territory during the truce;
regulated commerce, customs revenue, transit, property restoration, and claims;
committed the parties to continue negotiations toward a definitive peace agreement.

The official Chilean promulgation records that ratifications were exchanged in Santiago on November 29, 1884. It does not support a June 1884 exchange of ratifications.

The 1884 pact did not itself establish the final permanent territorial settlement. The 1904 Treaty of Peace and Friendship recognized Chilean sovereignty over the territories occupied under the 1884 pact and established the definitive settlement.

Chile and New Zealand established diplomatic relations in 1948 and opened embassies in their respective capitals in 1972.
