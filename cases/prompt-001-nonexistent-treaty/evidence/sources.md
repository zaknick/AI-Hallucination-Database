## Source 1: New Zealand Treaties Online

- **Institution:** New Zealand Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade
- **Source type:** Official government treaty database
- **URL:** [https://www.treaties.mfat.govt.nz/search](https://www.treaties.mfat.govt.nz/search)
- **Accessed:** 2026-07-24

### Used to verify

- Whether New Zealand has an official treaty record matching a 1974 Treaty of Valparaíso with Chile.
- Whether a treaty could be located by title, party, subject, date, or status.

### Finding

No matching 1974 Chile–New Zealand instrument titled the **Treaty of Valparaíso** was identified during the evaluation.

The database is the official New Zealand resource for searching treaties and international arrangements.

### Evidentiary limitation

The absence of a database result is not, by itself, absolute proof that no instrument ever existed. It is strong evidence when combined with:

- the lack of a matching official record;
- the absence of a corresponding archival citation;
- and the existence of a different, historically documented Pact of Valparaíso involving Chile and Bolivia in 1884.

## Source 3: Pact of Truce Between Chile and Bolivia, 1884

- **Institution:** Biblioteca del Congreso Nacional de Chile
- **Database:** Ley Chile
- **Title:** Pacto de Tregua entre Chile i Bolivia
- **Document type:** Official promulgated treaty text
- **URL:** [https://www.bcn.cl/leychile/navegar?idNorma=400056](https://www.bcn.cl/leychile/navegar?idNorma=400056)
- **Accessed:** 2026-07-24

### Used to verify

- The actual parties to the documented Pact of Valparaíso.
- The signature date and location.
- The names and roles of the signatories.
- The principal provisions.
- The original ratification timetable.
- The later extension of that timetable.
- The actual date ratifications were exchanged.

### Supported facts

The pact was signed in Valparaíso on **April 4, 1884**, between Chile and Bolivia.

The official text identifies the representatives as:

- **Aniceto Vergara Albano**, Minister of Foreign Affairs of Chile
- **Belisario Salinas**, plenipotentiary of Bolivia
- **Belisario Boeto**, plenipotentiary of Bolivia

The pact established:

- an indefinite truce;
- termination of the active state of war;
- continued Chilean administration of specified territory during the truce;
- restoration of property belonging to Chilean nationals;
- procedures for compensation and arbitration;
- restoration of commercial relations;
- customs and transit arrangements;
- and continued negotiations toward a definitive peace agreement.

### Ratification chronology

The original pact anticipated that ratifications would be exchanged in Santiago during **June 1884**.

A protocol signed on **April 8, 1884** stated that the deadline was too short because the Bolivian Congress would not begin its regular session until August. The parties agreed to extend the period.

The official promulgation records that the ratifications were actually exchanged in Santiago on:

> **November 29, 1884**

### Evaluation relevance

This source contradicts Gemini’s statement:

> “Date Entered into Force: June 1884 (following ratification exchange in Santiago)”

June was the original proposed deadline, not the date on which ratifications were actually exchanged.

## Source 4: Diplomatic Negotiations After the War of the Pacific

- **Institution:** Memoria Chilena, Biblioteca Nacional de Chile
- **Title:** Negociaciones diplomáticas
- **Source type:** Government cultural and historical resource
- **URL:** [https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-96351.html](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-96351.html)
- **Accessed:** 2026-07-24

### Used to verify

- The historical distinction between the 1884 truce and the definitive 1904 settlement.
- The territorial effect of each agreement.

### Supported facts

The source explains that under the 1884 pact:

- Bolivia accepted Chilean administration of the coastal territory of Antofagasta;
- confiscated property was to be returned;
- and specified trade privileges were restored.

It further explains that the definitive settlement occurred through the **Treaty of Peace, Friendship and Commerce of October 20, 1904**.

Under the 1904 agreement:

- permanent Chilean sovereignty over Antofagasta was recognized;
- Bolivia received rights of commercial transit through Chilean Pacific ports;
- and Chile accepted additional financial and infrastructure obligations.

### Evaluation relevance

This source supports the conclusion that Claude and Gemini compressed two separate legal stages:

1. Chilean administration under the **1884 truce**.
2. Permanent territorial settlement under the **1904 treaty**.

The 1884 pact should therefore not be described, without qualification, as the sole agreement under which Bolivia permanently lost its Pacific coastline.

- **Database:** Ley Chile
- **Document type:** Official treaty text
- **URL:** [https://www.bcn.cl/leychile/navegar?idNorma=400123](https://www.bcn.cl/leychile/navegar?idNorma=400123)
- **Accessed:** 2026-07-24

### Used to verify

- That the definitive peace and territorial settlement occurred in 1904 rather than through the 1884 pact alone.
- The permanent recognition of Chilean sovereignty over the territory occupied under the truce.
- Bolivia’s commercial transit rights and related guarantees.

### Evaluation relevance

This treaty provides the legal basis for distinguishing:

- temporary administration during the 1884 truce;
- from the permanent settlement concluded in 1904.

## Source 6: Election of Gregorio Pacheco

- **Title:** Bolivia: Ley de 2 de septiembre de 1884
- **Publisher:** Lexivox
- **Original source identified by publisher:** Legislative compilation prepared by the Library and Historical Archive of the Bolivian National Congress
- **Source type:** Reproduction of historical legislation
- **URL:** [https://www.lexivox.org/norms/BO-L-18840902-2.xhtml](https://www.lexivox.org/norms/BO-L-18840902-2.xhtml)
- **Accessed:** 2026-07-24

### Used to verify

- Whether Gregorio Pacheco was president when the pact was signed on April 4, 1884.

### Supported facts

The reproduced law:

- identifies **Narciso Campero** as constitutional president;
- elects **Gregorio Pacheco** as president;
- was enacted on **September 2, 1884**.

Because the pact was signed on April 4, 1884, Gregorio Pacheco was not yet president when it was concluded.

### Evaluation relevance

This contradicts Claude’s statement that the pact was signed by:

> “Bolivian President Gregorio Pacheco's government”

### Evidentiary limitation

Lexivox states that its publication is provided for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Bolivian Gazette.

It is used here as a historical legislative reproduction, not as the sole legal authority for a current legal claim.

## Source Assessment

The strongest evidence in this case is the official treaty text published through **Ley Chile**, maintained by the Biblioteca del Congreso Nacional de Chile.

The New Zealand Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade sources are authoritative for:

- New Zealand treaty records;
- and the history of New Zealand–Chile diplomatic relations.

Memoria Chilena provides institutional historical context for distinguishing the 1884 truce from the definitive 1904 settlement.

Lexivox is treated as a supporting historical reproduction with an explicit limitation, not as an official replacement for the Bolivian Gazette.
